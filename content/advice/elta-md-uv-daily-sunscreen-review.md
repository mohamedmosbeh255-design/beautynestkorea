## The active ingredients: what is actually protecting your skin

Ingredient transparency is the first real test of any sunscreen, and the authoritative source here is the FDA drug label published on DailyMed. According to that label:

- **Zinc Oxide 9.0%** in the SPF 40 version and **11.0%** in the SPF 50 version. This is a mineral filter that covers UVA and UVB simultaneously because it both scatters and absorbs UV radiation. At these percentages it is also the reason the formula is described as gentle on sensitive skin.
- **Octinoxate 7.5%** in the SPF 40 version. A chemical filter that strengthens UVB protection and is largely responsible for pushing the SPF rating to 40 while keeping the texture light.
- **Octisalate 5.0%** in the SPF 50 version. A substitute chemical booster in the newer formula, playing a similar supporting role.

### The mistake most reviews repeat: this is not a 100% mineral sunscreen

"With Zinc Oxide" on the front of the bottle does not mean the formula is mineral-only. Because it contains **Octinoxate** or **Octisalate**, the product is a **hybrid** sunscreen. Why that matters in practice:

- If you specifically need a pure 100% mineral sunscreen — for very reactive skin or personal preference — **EltaMD UV Daily** is not that product.
- If you want broad-spectrum mineral protection without the heavy, cast-prone feel of pure mineral formulas, the hybrid system is exactly why this one spreads so well.

## The three tints: which one should you pick?

In practice, choosing the right tint matters almost as much as choosing the sunscreen:

- **Untinted:** fully transparent with a subtle healthy glow. Best under makeup, ideal for fair to medium skin tones, and the safest pick for anyone who does not want a visible tint (including most men who dislike any cosmetic feel).
- **Tinted:** adds sheer coverage that evens tone and blurs mild redness or post-acne marks. For many people this is the most practical option because it can replace foundation on ordinary days. Note that the tinted version is priced higher — $47.00 versus $41.00 in the listing we tracked.
- **Deep/Dark Tinted:** a deeper shade built for medium-to-deep skin tones, where a light tint would otherwise produce the grey cast that gives tinted sunscreens a bad reputation on darker skin.

**Practical tip:** shade numbers on retail pages (for example "04 – Untinted") shift between sizes and packaging revisions, so confirm the shade by name rather than by number before checkout.

## SPF 40 or SPF 50? The honest difference

Many shoppers assume higher numbers automatically mean better protection and buy the top of the range. The reality is more precise:

| Factor | UV Daily SPF 40 | UV Daily Hydration+ SPF 50 |
|---|---|---|
| Actives | Zinc Oxide 9% + Octinoxate 7.5% | Zinc Oxide 11% + Octisalate 5% |
| Zinc level | 9% | 11% (slightly higher) |
| Positioning | Daily moisture for normal, dry, and combination skin | Positioned for dry and dehydrated skin, with extra emphasis on hydration |
| Real-world difference | SPF 40 blocks roughly 97.5% of UVB rays | SPF 50 blocks roughly 98% of UVB rays |

So the gap in actual UVB blocking between SPF 40 and SPF 50 is about **half a percentage point**. What actually decides your outcome is how much product you apply, whether you reapply every two hours, and whether you picked the version matching your skin type. If your skin feels tight after cleansing, the SPF 50 version is the logical choice. If you wear it under makeup or live in a hot, humid climate, the SPF 40 version often feels lighter and less tacky.

## How to use it properly (this is what determines the result)

The drug label is unambiguous, and ignoring these points is the number one reason people think a sunscreen "did not work":

1. Apply **liberally** to the face and neck **15 minutes before** sun exposure.
2. Practical face dose: about **two teaspoons** for face and neck, or roughly a **two-finger length** for the face alone.
3. Reapply **at least every two hours** during continuous exposure, and immediately after swimming, heavy sweating, or towel drying.
4. Do not rely on the product alone: limiting exposure between **10 a.m. and 2 p.m.** and wearing a hat and sunglasses reduces total UV load more than jumping from SPF 40 to SPF 50.
5. Sensitive skin: patch test behind the ear or on the forearm the night before first full use.
6. Wash it off at night. Tinted versions deserve a slightly more thorough removal step — a cleansing oil or balm followed by a regular cleanser — to avoid clogged pores.

### A key question: does a moisturizing sunscreen replace moisturizer?

If your skin is genuinely dry, usually not. A moisturizing sunscreen eases dryness but is not a substitute for an evening moisturizer built around **ceramides** or **squalane**. The better routine is a barrier-repairing moisturizer at night and EltaMD UV Daily in the morning for protection with comfortable hydration.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Is EltaMD UV Daily good for oily skin?**
It is usable, but it is not the ideal match. The base is rich in petrolatum and dimethicone, so it can feel heavy on very oily skin. For oily or acne-prone skin, EltaMD UV Clear SPF 46 or a lightweight watery sunscreen is the better direction.

**Is it a mineral sunscreen?**
No. The SPF 40 version pairs Zinc Oxide 9% with Octinoxate 7.5%, and the SPF 50 version pairs Zinc Oxide 11% with Octisalate 5%. That makes it a **hybrid** sunscreen.

**Is EltaMD UV Daily water resistant?**
No. The label advises using a water-resistant sunscreen for swimming or sweating, which confirms this formula is not water resistant. Reapply immediately after swimming, sweating, or towel drying.

**What is the difference between SPF 40 and SPF 50?**
The UVB-blocking difference is roughly half a percentage point. The meaningful differences are a higher zinc level in the SPF 50 version (11%) and its stronger emphasis on hydrating dry, dehydrated skin.

**Does it work under makeup?**
Yes. Apply it first and let it settle for two to three minutes before foundation. The untinted version is less likely to shift your foundation shade, while the tinted version can replace foundation on light days.

**Does it clog pores or cause breakouts?**
It is rated non-comedogenic and is fragrance-free, which lowers irritation risk. Response is still individual: any silicone-rich sunscreen can congest some pores, in which case thorough evening removal is the first fix.

**How often should I reapply?**
At least every two hours of direct, continuous exposure, and immediately after swimming, heavy sweating, or towel drying.

**Can children use it?**
The label advises consulting a physician before use on children **under 6 months**. For older children, a simple mineral sunscreen formulated for kids is usually the better choice than an adult moisturizing sunscreen.
