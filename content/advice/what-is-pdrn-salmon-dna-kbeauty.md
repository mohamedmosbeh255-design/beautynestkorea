PDRN (polydeoxyribonucleotide) is a DNA-derived ingredient, most famously sourced from salmon or trout, long used in Korean dermatology clinics — in its injectable form. In K-beauty it has jumped from the clinic into serums, creams and sheet masks marketed for hydration, glow and a firmer, bouncier look.

Why the hype? PDRN is prized for supporting the skin barrier, improving the look of elasticity and firmness, calming stressed skin, and boosting hydration — the exact combo behind the glass-skin finish. It pairs well with hydrators like hyaluronic acid, collagen and niacinamide.

**What the evidence actually supports.** Most published PDRN research studied the injectable form in clinical settings; there are no published randomized trials of a topical PDRN serum used alone on intact skin. Treat topical PDRN as a cosmetic: realistic expectations are better-looking hydration, glow and firmness with consistent use — not scar, wrinkle or "repair" treatment. That gap between clinic and shelf is exactly why reading the label matters.

**How to read a PDRN label (the ppm trap).** In the INCI list the real ingredient reads *Polydeoxyribonucleotide*; many products marketed as "PDRN" actually list *Sodium DNA*, a broader salt — in one 2026 audit of 188 products labeled PDRN, 171 listed Sodium DNA and none listed polydeoxyribonucleotide. Treat headline numbers like "10,000 ppm PDRN" as marketing math: disclosed PDRN levels in real formulas run far lower, and leave-on OTC serums commonly sit around 0.5–2%. Also check the source: salmon/trout-derived PDRN and vegan, plant-derived alternatives (some brands use ginseng-derived PDRN) are different molecules with no proven equivalence — pick one lane and don't expect identical results.

How to use it: apply PDRN serums or masks on clean, slightly damp skin, 2–4 times per week. Layer lightest to heaviest (toner → PDRN serum → moisturizer), and always finish mornings with SPF. If you are sensitive or acne-prone, patch-test first and introduce one PDRN product at a time. In a routine with actives: vitamin C first in the morning, wait 1–3 minutes, then PDRN; keep retinol at night (or wait about 5 minutes between them); strong AHAs/BHAs on alternate nights rather than in the same layer.

What to look for: formulas that combine PDRN with barrier helpers (ceramides, panthenol, hyaluronic acid), fragrance-free options for sensitive skin, and single-use sheet masks for a weekly glow boost. Compare prices on Amazon vs Olive Young — masks are often cheaper in multi-packs.

**A note on safety:** PDRN is DNA-derived — usually salmon or trout — so if you have a fish allergy, ask your clinician before trying it; published guidance conflicts on this. There are no safety trials in pregnancy or breastfeeding, so ask your clinician first. Patch-test any new product, and introduce one PDRN product at a time.
