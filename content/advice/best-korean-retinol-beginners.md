## How to start retinol without irritation

Beginners do not get irritated because retinol is "too strong" — they get irritated because they go too fast. Here is the protocol I recommend:

- **Weeks 1-2:** Apply a pea-size amount every other evening, on dry skin, after cleansing and before your moisturizer. Dry skin first — applying to damp skin can increase penetration and irritation.
- **Week 3 onward:** If your skin is comfortable (mild tightness is fine; peeling and stinging are not), move to nightly use.
- **Every morning:** Apply sunscreen. This is non-negotiable with vitamin A, because it can make skin more sun-sensitive.
- **Buffering:** Pair your retinal or retinol with panthenol and cica in the rest of your routine — these help support the skin barrier and reduce dryness. The innisfree pick above has cica built in.
- **Do not mix on the same night:** Alternate acids (AHAs/BHAs) with your retinol — for example, retinol on Monday, an AHA or BHA on Tuesday, retinol on Wednesday. Using both on the same evening is the most common beginner mistake after going nightly too soon.

## Concentration guide

Korean brands label vitamin A clearly, which helps beginners compare. A quick map:

- **Retinal 0.05-0.1%** — the beginner sweet spot. Retinal generally requires fewer conversion steps in the skin than retinol, and the celimax booster is a common, well-tolerated starting point.
- **Retinol 0.3-0.5%** — the step-up zone. Once you have tolerated a low-strength product nightly for several weeks without irritation, a 0.3-0.5% retinol like the COSRX oil is a sensible next bottle.
- **Bakuchiol** — for very sensitive skin or anyone who cannot tolerate vitamin A at all, bakuchiol is a plant-derived alternative. It is not a retinol, but it is often discussed as a gentler option. None of the four picks here are bakuchiol-based — this list is specifically retinal and retinol.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will I purge when starting retinol?**

Some beginners experience a short adjustment period with small breakouts or mild flaking in the first two to four weeks — this is often called purging, as skin cell turnover speeds up. It usually settles within a few weeks. Stinging that lasts, peeling that does not stop, or a rash is not purging — stop use and let your skin recover before restarting more slowly, and see a clinician if it persists.

**Can sensitive or acne-prone skin use these?**

Yes, with the right pick and pace. The innisfree Retinol Cica Moisture Recovery Serum is the one I recommend for sensitive skin because the cica and moisture-recovery base help buffer the retinol. Start every other night, keep the rest of your routine simple, and do not layer acids on the same evening.

**Can I use the Beauty of Joseon eye serum on my face?**

Treat it as a gentle facial retinal serum and keep it away from the immediate lash line and eyelids. If it is your only retinal product, the per-ml price ($0.57/ml) makes that a very economical choice.

**How long until I see results?**

Plan on 8-12 weeks of consistent use before judging results, and take progress photos in the same light to compare fairly. Retinoids work gradually — first you may notice smoother-looking texture, and the look of fine lines improves more slowly. If you see nothing at all after three months of nightly use, the strength may be too low for your skin, and a step-up like the COSRX 0.5 oil could be the next move.
