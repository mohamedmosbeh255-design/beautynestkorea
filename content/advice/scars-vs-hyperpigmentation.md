## 🎯 The 30-Second Answer

**Is your mark smooth and flat?** → It's hyperpigmentation (PIH). Good news: it often fades with sunscreen + time + the right serum.

**Is it indented or raised?** → It's a scar. Hard truth: no cream fills a scar. Silicone helps raised ones slightly; depressed scars need a dermatologist (microneedling/laser).

**The one rule that matters most:** Wear sunscreen every single day. Without it, nothing else works.

## 🔍 The 10-Second Test: Scar or Dark Spot?

1. **Run your finger over the mark.** Smooth = pigment. Bumpy or indented = scar.
2. **Look in side light** (phone flashlight from the side). Casts a shadow = scar.
3. **Press gently.** Fades white then returns = red mark (PIE). Stays brown = PIH [[127]].

That's it. You now know which path to take. 👇

##  A Simple Routine

**Morning:** Gentle cleanser → one active (Vitamin C *or* Niacinamide *or* Azelaic Acid) → moisturiser → broad-spectrum SPF 30+ (tinted/iron-oxide if you have a deeper skin tone [[68]]).

**Evening:** Cleanser → one active (a retinoid *or* Azelaic Acid — not both when starting) → moisturiser.

### The Core Difference — Structure vs Pigment
A **scar** is damaged collagen rebuilt imperfectly — too much (raised) or too little (depressed). You can feel it; it casts a shadow [[2]][[74]]. **Hyperpigmentation** leaves the surface intact — only melanin changes. It's flat and smooth [[9]][[23]]. That's why the finger-and-side-light test works.

### Raised vs Depressed Scars
**Hypertrophic scars** stay within the wound border; **keloids** grow beyond it and recur easily [[74]]. Silicone gel/sheets *may* slightly soften raised scars, but Cochrane rates the evidence low-to-very-low certainty [[10]][[11]] — and silicone does nothing for depressed scars [[16]]. Depressed scars (rolling, boxcar, ice-pick [[2]][[5]]) need procedures: microneedling has the best evidence as monotherapy [[105]][[110]], plus fractional lasers, subcision, fillers, and TCA CROSS [[1]][[3]][[70]]. All carry risks — including new PIH in darker skin [[1]][[4]].

### Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)
Excess pigment after inflammation — acne, eczema, friction. Most persistent in melanin-rich skin [[23]][[27]]. Step one: **stop picking**. Evidence-backed topicals: **retinoids** (tretinoin/adapalene/trifarotene) [[19]][[22]][[27]]; **azelaic acid 15–20%** [[44]][[47]][[50]] (try our [Anua Azelaic Acid 10 serum](/product/anua-azelaic-acid-10-hyaluron-serum)); **niacinamide 4–5%** [[52]][[55]]; **vitamin C 10–20%** [[113]][[117]]; **tranexamic acid 3–5%** [[85]][[89]]. **Hydroquinone** is potent but risks permanent ochronosis — medical supervision only [[78]][[79]][[83]].

**PIE vs PIH:** press the mark — blanches white = PIE (vascular); stays = PIH (pigment) [[127]]. PIE needs soothing + vascular lasers; PIH needs pigment actives + SPF [[122]].

### Sunscreen Is Non-Negotiable
UV darkens pigment and slows fading [[9]][[63]]. Crucially, in deeper skin tones **visible light** also drives long-lasting pigment — tinted/iron-oxide sunscreens protect meaningfully better than untinted [[61]][[64]][[68]]. SPF doesn't "heal" scars; it prevents worsening so everything else can work.

### Purging vs Irritation
"Purging" is overused. True purging (faster surfacing of existing microcomedones) peaks in 2–6 weeks in usual breakout zones [[30]][[35]]. Most "purging" is actually **irritation** — stinging, diffuse redness, new-area bumps [[28]][[33]]. If it stings week after week, slow down — don't push through. The AAD advises starting retinoids low and slow, especially in skin of colour [[29]].

### Nasolabial Folds & Deep Wrinkles
Topicals cannot lift or fill a nasolabial fold — it's driven by volume loss. Moisturisers plump temporarily; retinoids improve fine surface texture only [[19]][[28]]. Real change is procedural (fillers, RF/ultrasound, resurfacing) [[70]] — legitimate, but medical decisions, not skincare upgrades.

### Facial & Neck Exercises
Evidence is thin and mixed. Small studies suggest possible modest cheek fullness [[36]][[38]], but systematic reviews find **insufficient evidence** for rejuvenation [[40]][[43]]. Repetitive platysma contraction could theoretically deepen lines [[36]]. Low-risk for most, but not an evidence-based anti-ageing treatment — don't swap it for sunscreen and proven care.

## 💡 Key Takeaways
- **Scars = structural; hyperpigmentation = pigment.** Smooth/flat = pigment; textured/indented = scar.
- **PIH isn't a scar** — excess melanin that often fades with SPF + time + right actives.
- **PIE (red) ≠ PIH (brown)** — the blanch test tells them apart.
- **Sunscreen is non-negotiable** — tinted/iron-oxide matters for deeper skin (visible light drives pigment).
- **Topicals can't fill depressed scars or lift deep folds** — those need procedures.
- **"Purging" is often irritation** — persistent sting means slow down.
- **Facial exercises lack strong evidence** — not a substitute for SPF + proven care.
- **Hydroquinone is powerful but risky** — medical supervision only.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I tell a scar from hyperpigmentation?** Finger + side light: smooth/flat = PIH; indented/raised = scar [[127]].

**Will hyperpigmentation fade on its own?** Epidermal PIH often fades over months with SPF; dermal pigment takes longer [[9]][[128]].

**Does vitamin C lighten dark spots?** L-ascorbic acid 10–20% has clinical support, but stability matters [[113]][[117]].

**Is azelaic acid safe for darker skin?** Yes — well tolerated, effective for PIH/melasma, plus anti-acne [[46]][[47]].

**Breakouts after retinol — purging?** Maybe if in usual zones and settling in weeks. Persistent sting/redness = irritation — slow down [[28]][[30]].

**Can a cream fill indented scars?** No. Retinoids mildly improve texture; real correction needs procedures [[2]][[105]].

**Do facial exercises reverse ageing?** Limited, mixed evidence — insufficient to confirm benefits [[40]][[43]].

**When should I see a dermatologist?** Changing, painful, severe, keloid-like, or >6–12 months persistent — book a consult [[69]][[70]].
