Think of the 10-step Korean skincare routine as a customizable menu, not a rule you must follow every day. It was popularized by Soko Glam founder Charlotte Cho in 2012 as a personalization framework, a way to pick only the layers your skin actually needs. The core idea is simple: apply lightweight layers first, then thicker creams, and finish mornings with sunscreen. In this guide you get the full order, a realistic AM and PM split, safe frequencies, and a start-with-4 path.

## The 4-step beginner core

You do not need ten products to start. A simple core covers most of what skin needs: a gentle cleanser, a hydrating toner, a moisturizer, and a broad-spectrum SPF 30+ in the morning. Cleveland Clinic dermatologist Dr. Alok Vij does not recommend a 10-step routine to patients, according to Cleveland Clinic, Nov 2023. Dr. Jane Yoo says a 4-step routine done daily outperforms a 10-step routine done occasionally. Koreabe, Mar 30, 2025, calls this the Core Four. A minimal morning can be as short as cleanser, then moisturizer, then sunscreen. Start here for two to three weeks. If skin feels calm and comfortable, you have already won. Add extra steps only for a specific concern, such as extra dryness or visible dark spots.

## The 10 steps, in order (thin to thick)

Work from thinnest to thickest so light layers can sit close to skin and heavier layers lock in hydration above them.

1. **Oil cleanser (evening) — Daily on makeup or sunscreen days.** Like dissolves like: oil melts makeup and sunscreen that water alone can miss. Massage onto dry skin, then add lukewarm water to emulsify and rinse.
2. **Water-based cleanser — Morning and evening.** Removes sweat and dirt left behind after the first cleanse. The AAD advises washing with fingertips and lukewarm water, at most twice daily plus after sweating.
3. **Exfoliant — 1–2x/week maximum.** Never daily for most beginners, per Healthline, Mar 2021, and Cleveland Clinic, Nov 2023. Dr. Joshua Zeichner says about twice weekly for maintenance is enough. Exact frequency depends on skin type and method, per the AAD.
4. **Toner — Daily.** A hydrating first layer applied on damp skin. Brands describe some formulas as pH-balancing, which is a brand-described claim rather than a proven effect.
5. **Essence — Daily, if wanted.** A lightweight hydrating layer between toner and serum. Brands describe support for cell turnover, which again is brand-described language, not an independent finding.
6. **Treatment serum or ampoule — Daily, one concern at a time.** Use vitamin C in the morning and a retinoid at night if you use them, and introduce each slowly.
7. **Sheet mask — 1–2x/week.** Leave on for 15–20 minutes, not daily. Dr. Jane Yoo notes that mask ingredients do not penetrate better than a well-formulated serum. Most guides place masks before moisturizer, while a few, including K Beauty World, place them before treatments. When guides conflict, thin-to-thick is the rule.
8. **Eye cream — Daily, optional.** A small, targeted moisturizer for the eye area. Many people find their regular moisturizer does the same job.
9. **Moisturizer — Morning and evening.** Seals in everything above it. Occlusives, humectants, and emollients support the skin barrier, according to a peer-reviewed review published in Karger.
10. **Sunscreen (morning only, final step) — Daily.** Use a broad-spectrum SPF 30+, per the AAD. SPF 15 filters about 93% of UVB and SPF 30 about 97%, per the AAD, accessed 2026-10-04. Use about one half teaspoon for face and neck and about one ounce for head to toe. Apply 15 minutes before sun exposure and reapply at least every 2 hours, per FDA and AAD guidance. Up to 80% of UV rays penetrate clouds, per the AAD.

## AM vs PM: nobody does 10 steps twice a day

Beginner communities keep repeating that no one has time for 10 steps twice a day, and they are right. A realistic morning is 4–6 steps. The full ritual fits a few evenings a week. Double cleansing belongs in the evening, or on days you wore makeup or waterproof sunscreen.

| Morning | Evening |
|---|---|
| Water-based cleanser | Oil cleanser |
| Toner | Water-based cleanser |
| Essence | Exfoliant (1–2x/week) |
| Serum | Toner |
| Moisturizer | Essence |
| Sunscreen | Serum |
|  | Sheet mask (1–2x/week; swap it in place of the serum, both are not required) |
|  | Eye cream |
|  | Moisturizer |

If your skin feels tight after two cleanses in the morning, drop the morning cleanser to lukewarm water. If an evening already includes an exfoliant, skip the sheet mask that night. Mornings should always end with sunscreen as the last layer.

## How long should I wait between steps?

Short answer: you can keep going without long pauses. As community practice rather than strict rules, many guides suggest these ranges: no waiting is fine; allow 1–2 minutes between hydrating layers; allow 5–10 minutes after chemical exfoliants; leave a sheet mask on for 15–20 minutes and follow the label; give sunscreen 5–10 minutes to set before makeup. Answers conflict even among guides, so treat these as flexible ranges.

## The frequency myth-buster

- **Double cleanse every day?** Fine at night if you wore makeup or waterproof SPF. If skin feels tight or dry, oil-cleanse only as needed, based on community reports, which are anecdotal. One small 20-person test, as of 2019, found cleansing oil left 5.8% waterproof-SPF residue versus 36.8% for a foaming cleanser alone. For non-waterproof SPF the gap was small, 13.4% versus 15.6%. The evidence is narrow: one small study.
- **Exfoliate daily for glow?** No. Keep it to 1–2x/week maximum for most people, and dermatologist guidance goes no higher than 2–3x/week. The AAD notes that exact frequency depends on skin type and method. Stop if you see redness, burning, or peeling. Avoid harsh exfoliation on darker skin tones where dark marks appear after irritation, per the AAD.
- **Sheet mask every day?** Not needed. Use a sheet mask 1–2x/week or as desired, unless the label says otherwise. A simple serum plus moisturizer supports hydrated, smoother-looking skin on other nights.
- **Glass skin in 2 weeks?** Glass skin is a beauty term, not a medical diagnosis. Airbrushed, heavily filtered images are not real skin. In research presented Sep 30, 2026, 94.7%, or 36 of 38, viral K-beauty glass skin TikTok videos contained at least one potentially misleading claim. Aim instead for hydrated, smoother-looking skin with steady habits.
- **Shrink your pores?** Pore size is mostly genetic. You cannot permanently shrink pores, but gentle cleansing, daily sun protection, and safe exfoliation can help pores look less noticeable, per the AAD and Cleveland Clinic. Skip any product that promises to close or erase pores.

## How to expand from 4 steps to 10

Start with the Core Four for a few weeks, then add only one new product every 2–4 weeks. That slow pace tells you which product caused a change, good or bad. Patch-test each newcomer first. The AAD patch-test method is a quarter-sized spot applied twice daily for 7–10 days, and if the spot turns red, itchy, or swollen, do not use the product. Actives like vitamin C or retinoids need roughly 4–6 weeks to show an effect, according to dermatologist estimates. Going slow is how you learn what actually works for your skin.

## What the evidence backs vs. what's optional

The strongest evidence supports the basics: gentle cleansing, moisturizing, and daily broad-spectrum SPF, per AAD and FDA guidance. No published trial compares a full 10-step routine with a 3-step one, so no one can honestly claim 10 beats 3, or 3 beats 10. Essences, serums, sheet masks, and eye cream are optional layers whose benefits are largely brand-described. Consistency with a small routine beats an occasional full ritual.

## FAQ

### Do I need to do all 10 steps every day as a beginner?

No. Start with 4 to 5 steps and add more only if you enjoy the ritual or have a specific concern. One community poster reports a Korean dermatologist saying most Koreans use 3–5 steps, presented here explicitly as an anecdote rather than a statistic. Consistency with 4 to 5 steps beats an occasional full 10-step session.

### How often should I double cleanse?

Double cleanse in the evening, or on days you wore makeup or waterproof sunscreen. If your skin feels tight or dry, reduce oil cleansing to only those days. See the frequency myth-buster section above, since needs vary by skin type.

### What order do toner, essence and serum go in — and where does the sheet mask fit?

Go thin to thick: toner first, then essence, then serum. Place the sheet mask before moisturizer, swapping it in place of the serum on mask nights since both are not required. Guides disagree on mask placement, so thin-to-thick wins when advice conflicts.

### How long should I wait between steps?

No waiting is also fine for most layers. Use 1–2 minutes between hydrating layers, 5–10 minutes after chemical exfoliants, 15–20 minutes for a sheet mask per the label, and 5–10 minutes for sunscreen to set before makeup. These are community-practice ranges, not strict rules.

### I have sensitive skin — where do I start?

Start with the 4-step core only: a gentle cleanser, a simple moisturizer, and daily SPF, plus toner if wanted. Patch-test each product using the AAD method above and add only one newcomer every few weeks. Add actives like vitamin C or retinoids only after skin feels comfortable and calm.

**A note on safety:** this is cosmetic guidance and product curation, not medical advice. Patch-test every new product using the AAD method above, introduce one product at a time, and stop use if redness, burning, or peeling appears. See a dermatologist for persistent acne, eczema, or irritation.
